Nigel Patch · Composition in development

Nigelæn
Symphony Project

How much music can grow from one small idea? Patch is developing a symphonic work in which a phrase can pass from a human voice to electronics and return in the sound of an orchestra.

The proposed ensemble

Duration
14–16 minutes
Forces
Full orchestra, electronics, and occasional solo soprano
Scale
About 70–80 performers, with a possible 30–35-player chamber version

One idea for the ear to follow

Composers call a short pattern of notes or rhythms a cell. Think of a phrase you recognize even when someone hums it slowly. Patch wants to find out how much further that recognition can stretch. The spaces between a few notes might shape a bass line, the timing of a rhythm, or even how long an entire section lasts.

The dramatic plan puts that idea under pressure. The orchestra gathers momentum until a rupture leaves room for a lone voice. As the ensemble rebuilds, the voice and electronics begin to sound like extensions of one another. Patch wants the piece to end just as the listener can imagine where it might go next.

Where does the voice go?

Imagine one sustained note making the following journey. This listening example illustrates a possibility in the composition brief.

  1. The voice

    A soprano holds a note long enough for its pitch to settle in your ear.

  2. The aftersound

    She takes a breath. Electronics stretch the sound into a cloud that hangs in the space she has left.

  3. The low register

    The same pitch returns much lower in the electronic bass. The brass gather around it to form a chord.

  4. The pulse

    The brass repeat the chord in short bursts. What began as a single breath now sets the orchestra in motion.

What the composer decides

A numerical relationship can suggest when the soprano might enter. It takes an ear to decide whether she should arrive on the beat or a breath later. That distinction guides Patch’s approach to authorship. He sets the dramatic goals and chooses the timing, instrumental colors, and ideas that survive revision.

He plans to use AI to help audition alternatives, compare orchestrations, and flag passages that may sit awkwardly in an instrument’s range. A reading with musicians would let him hear which choices hold up in the room.

From brief to first reading

The composition brief sets out the ensemble, the dramatic arc, and the experiment with a musical cell. The planned next materials are a conductor’s score, individual parts, an electronics cue score, and a virtual mock-up to hear how they fit together. A live reading and a chamber adaptation are longer-term possibilities.
