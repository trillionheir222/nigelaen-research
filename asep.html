ASEP

Advanced Symbiotic Enhancements and Protections

ASEP is Nigel Patch’s design study of how technology could assist human movement, respond to a person’s intent, and monitor how their body responds. It asks how these capabilities could work together while the person retains control over the assistance they receive.

The design questionSubjects coveredThe written record

The design question

If a person asks for help with a movement, what happens when they change their mind halfway through?

Starting and stopping assistance

Consider someone reaching for an object with mechanical assistance. They might decide to let go before the movement is complete. Their original intention, their body’s response, and their decision to stop each matter at a different moment.

This illustration gets at what “protections” means in ASEP. The design documents treat consent, the ability to withdraw it, and the consequences of doing so as questions to address alongside the capability itself.

Subjects covered in the design study

Movement and mechanical assistance

The study considers how a device could support physical action and what the wearer would need to know about the help it was providing.

Neural interfaces

Patch examines how a person could communicate an intention to an assistive system. A central concern is who has authority to start, change, or end an action.

Physiological monitoring

The documents consider what changes in the body might tell a person about their response to assistance, and when those changes might warrant a pause or review.

The written record

3,325 pages of private design documentation

What exists today

Patch’s compiled volume includes a program overview, proposed designs, requirements, and a glossary. Across those materials, he considers how several forms of augmentation might interact and what safeguards each would require.

The documented milestone is the design study. Whether a particular proposal can work safely in practice remains an open question. The technical manuscripts and individual designs are private.
