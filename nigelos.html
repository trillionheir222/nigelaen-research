NigelOS

Nigel Patch gave AI agents different jobs to see whether a group of specialists could carry a task from one person’s request to a usable result. The project taught him how much of that work happens during the handoff between jobs.

What Patch tried

A request could be divided among specialists, each with its own instructions. Some parts ran within a working session. Across sessions, decisions and unfinished work depended on a written record that had to be kept current. Patch eventually archived the experiment.

The handoff problem

Imagine one specialist drafts a research note and another spots an unsupported claim. Someone has to return the note for revision, preserve the reason for the change, and check the corrected version. Writing “reviewer” on a job description settles none of those questions.

This example captures a lesson from NigelOS. Dividing a task is relatively easy. Making responsibility clear when work changes hands takes design of its own.

What carried forward

NigelOS sharpened the questions Patch brought to later work. What does the next person or agent need to know? Which decision should survive into a new session? Who can judge that the work is ready? Those questions about coordination and memory are the reason he keeps NigelOS in the engineering archive.
