PatchOS

What would have to stay true for a possible future to remain within reach? Nigel Patch developed PatchOS to explore how a computer might keep several possibilities in view and revise them as evidence changes.

A computational “crystal ball”

Patch’s original image for the project was a crystal ball. What interested him was the part a forecast usually leaves out. If two outcomes both seem plausible, which assumption separates them? What new observation would give someone a reason to change their mind?

An explanation can survive every check so far and still be wrong. Keeping its assumptions visible gives a researcher somewhere to look next.

Two routes before dusk

Imagine a field team trying to reach an island before dusk. It can take the ferry or walk across a causeway at low tide. A cancellation rules out the ferry. The causeway remains a possibility, but only if the tide stays low long enough for the crossing.

Learning that the causeway was open tells us the team could have crossed. To say it did cross requires another observation. This imagined case illustrates the distinction PatchOS was designed to preserve.

Research status

PatchOS exists as a private research prototype. Its purpose is to help a researcher keep track of competing explanations as the available evidence changes.

How well that approach works is a question for evaluation. A useful test would ask whether a researcher can tell which assumption changed a conclusion and what evidence would justify changing it again.

Where the question leads

In scientific research, two explanations may fit the same evidence. A useful reasoning aid could help a researcher identify the observation that would distinguish them. That is one of the applications Patch is investigating.

A related question is how to turn information into useful judgment. Patch explores that question in his Theory of Regulated Intelligence Flow. PatchOS gives that question a place in his experimental engineering work.
