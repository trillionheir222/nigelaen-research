Historical research architecture · Nigel Patch

PatchOS

The same signal can mean different things in different settings. Nigel Patch developed PatchOS to explore how a computer could recognize a shared pattern, keep track of the circumstances around it, and hold several possible interpretations open.

One bell, two meanings

Imagine hearing a school bell. In one classroom, the students close their books and leave. In another, students arriving from the corridor take their seats. A recording of the bell alone gives you little reason to choose between those two scenes.

That is the kind of ambiguity PatchOS was designed to preserve. The bell is the shared pattern. What happened before it rang gives the pattern context. The two scenes suggest different continuations. Recognizing the sound is only part of understanding what might happen next.

What disagreement can tell you

Patch called the divergence between possible continuations tension. The interesting question was what the system should do with it. If two contexts point in different directions, recording where they diverge can be useful in its own right. Choosing an outcome or changing what the system remembers is a separate decision.

The historical record preserves that question as it developed, including revisions, abandoned branches, and competing answers. Those alternatives show which choices were still open at each stage of the design.

What the archive establishes

Selected parts of the early architecture were reconstructed and checked with fixed, synthetic inputs. In one such check, a reconstructed piece took a supplied description of disagreement and returned an observation while leaving the input intact.

These checks document what the reconstructed pieces did under specified conditions. Judging whether a full system could anticipate a real event would require a different experiment.

Its place in the larger system

Patch describes PatchOS as a component of a larger cognitive system he has since built. He keeps the wider system’s design and operation private. The PatchOS archive gives readers a way to examine how one of its underlying questions arose. What should a system remember about circumstances in which more than one interpretation made sense?

Patch groups this work under Confidential cognitive systems R&D. His Theory of Regulated Intelligence Flow takes up a related question about when the information a system holds becomes useful for a decision.
