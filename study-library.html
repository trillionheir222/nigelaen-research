Mathematical language

What stays the same when the coordinates change?

Draw an arrow on paper and rotate the grid beneath it. The numbers describing the arrow change, even though the arrow still points the same way. The mathematics guides use distinctions like this to connect notation with the objects it describes. The Greek alphabet companion makes a similar point on a smaller scale. The same letter can mean different things in different equations.

Linear Algebra Done RightSheldon Axler

Information theory

How can a noisy channel carry a reliable message?

Suppose a sender uses 000 for “off” and 111 for “on.” If the receiver sees 101 and knows that at most one bit changed, it can recover “on.” The extra bits cost something, but they let the receiver correct an error. That tradeoff is an entry point into coding theory and Shannon’s work on communication.

A Mathematical Theory of Communication (PDF)Claude E. Shannon

Dynamics & control

Why does a correction sometimes make things worse?

Picture a shower whose temperature responds a few seconds after you turn the tap. Before the first adjustment takes effect, you turn it again and overshoot. The dynamics and control guides ask how delay and the size of a correction affect whether a system settles or keeps oscillating.

Feedback SystemsKarl J. Åström and Richard M. Murray

Inference & model validation

What would earn a model your confidence?

A curve can pass through every measurement used to fit it. Set aside some observations before fitting, and you can ask how well the curve predicts them. The statistics and scientific computing guides also ask how accurately the calculation solves the equations and how well those equations describe the thing being measured.

An Introduction to Statistical LearningGareth James and coauthors

Strategy & character

Which part of a decision belongs to you?

You can prepare a talk carefully and still meet a skeptical audience. Epictetus asks which part of that experience is yours to direct. The personal study workbook sets that question alongside Machiavelli on reading circumstances, Marcus Aurelius on judgment, and Seneca on the use of time.

The EnchiridionEpictetus, translated by Elizabeth Carter
